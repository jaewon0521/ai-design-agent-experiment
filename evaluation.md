# Evaluation

## Objective

Measure how different AI design methodologies influence the final UI.

The goal is not to determine which methodology is objectively the best.

The goal is to identify how each methodology influences:

- design decisions
- visual direction
- information hierarchy
- interaction patterns
- implementation process

---

# Evaluation Criteria

## 1. AI Slop

Evaluate whether the UI relies on generic patterns commonly produced by AI-generated interfaces.

Consider:

- Generic layout
- Repeated card patterns
- Generic typography
- Generic color palette
- Excessive gradients
- Excessive rounded corners
- Generic SaaS patterns
- Meaningless decorative elements

Score:

1 — Strongly feels generic / AI-generated

2 — Noticeably generic

3 — Mixed

4 — Some distinctive characteristics

5 — Clearly distinctive

---

## 2. Domain Fit

Evaluate whether the interface appropriately represents a team-management product.

Consider:

- Does the UI reflect the needs of a team manager?
- Is information hierarchy appropriate?
- Does the interface support team-management workflows?
- Are important team-related information and actions easy to understand?

Score:

1 — Poor

2 — Weak

3 — Adequate

4 — Strong

5 — Excellent

---

## 3. Visual Identity

Evaluate whether the product establishes a recognizable visual direction.

Consider:

- Is there a clear visual direction?
- Are typography, color, spacing, and components coherent?
- Does the interface feel interchangeable with another SaaS product?
- Does the interface have memorable characteristics?

Score:

1 — No clear identity

2 — Weak identity

3 — Moderate identity

4 — Strong identity

5 — Distinctive identity

---

## 4. UX

Evaluate the usability and information architecture of the interface.

Consider:

- Is the information hierarchy clear?
- Are important actions easy to discover?
- Are states understandable?
- Can the user understand what to do without unnecessary exploration?

Score:

1 — Poor

2 — Weak

3 — Adequate

4 — Strong

5 — Excellent

---

## 5. Consistency

Evaluate consistency throughout the interface.

Consider:

- Typography
- Spacing
- Color
- Components
- Interaction patterns
- Visual hierarchy

Score:

1 — Inconsistent

2 — Noticeably inconsistent

3 — Adequate

4 — Consistent

5 — Highly consistent

---

# Evaluation Rules

- Evaluate all experiment groups using the same criteria.
- Do not evaluate one result relative to another during the first evaluation.
- Evaluate each result independently before comparing them.
- Do not reward a methodology simply because it produced more features.
- Do not penalize a methodology simply because its visual style differs from personal preference.
- Separate personal aesthetic preference from usability and domain fit.
- Record concrete observations supporting each score.

# Qualitative Observations

For each experiment record:

- What design decisions were notable?
- What patterns appeared repeatedly?
- What felt generic?
- What felt distinctive?
- What design methodology appears to have influenced the result?
- What trade-offs were introduced?
